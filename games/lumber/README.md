# games/lumber

**Lumber Camp** (working title), a solo stack-and-serve tycoon run as one **4-minute shift**. The sawmill turns logs
into planks on its tray; walk over the tray to stack planks on your back, drop them on the counter, and stand at the
till to sell to the builders queueing up. Their cash piles up beside the counter: walk over it to pocket it, then stand
on an unlock pad to spend it. Score = money earned in the shift (board `earnings`). Customers get busier as the shift
goes on and walk out when they wait too long, so the upgrades are what keep up with the rush. Portrait, PlayCanvas,
orthographic camera, low-poly primitives plus CC0 Quaternius characters and props (see Models).

A multiplayer mode (2–4 players sharing one camp for a shift) is planned. The rules are written for it already (see
below), but nothing networked exists yet.

```bash
pnpm --filter @rally/game-lumber dev        # http://localhost:5195 with the dev harness (fake host); ?nohost = standalone
pnpm --filter @rally/game-lumber build      # -> client/dist
pnpm --filter @rally/game-lumber test       # simulation unit tests (node:test)
pnpm --filter @rally/game-lumber test:e2e   # Playwright through the dev harness (needs Chromium; playwright via NODE_PATH)
pnpm --filter @rally/game-lumber models     # rebuild client/src/models/ from the art-source release (Models)
pnpm --filter @rally/game-lumber art        # re-render the store art in art/ (Artwork)
pnpm --filter @rally/publish-game publish-game lumber --dry-run
```

Controls: drag anywhere for a floating joystick (touch or mouse), or arrow keys / WASD.

In the Rally app, with the dev server running on a PC the phone can reach:

- **Developer screen** (anyone): Profile → Developer, bundle URL `http://<pc-ip>:5195/index.html`. The room does not
  matter (a solo game never matchmakes); saves and boards stay in memory under the dev identity.
- **Catalog** (owner, before the real publish): apply `docs/dev/lumber-dev-rows.sql` in Supabase Studio with the IP of
  the PC serving the game. Lumber Camp then shows up with its board and achievements; the real publish replaces it.

## Layout

| File                     | What                                                                                                          |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `game.json`              | Manifest: solo, portrait, boards `earnings` and `rush` (desc, ≤ $3,000/min), six achievements.                |
| `client/src/sim.ts`      | The rules and the camp layout, engine-free and deterministic (below). Unit-tested in `test/sim.test.ts`.      |
| `client/src/progress.ts` | Modes, stars and unlocks across shifts, and the save slot's shape (below). Tested in `test/progress.test.ts`. |
| `client/src/bot.ts`      | A greedy player: drives the unit tests' economy checks, the e2e, and balancing.                               |
| `client/src/scene.ts`    | PlayCanvas rendering: camp, mills, piles of planks and cash, people, pads, camera.                            |
| `client/src/models.ts`   | Loads `client/src/models/` (characters, clips, props), generated by `scripts/models.ts`.                      |
| `client/src/labels.ts`   | World-anchored DOM labels: pad prices, customer orders + patience, FULL / MAX, floating money.                |
| `client/src/controls.ts` | Floating joystick + keys (same shape as Sumo's).                                                              |
| `client/src/main.ts`     | Boot, HUD, tutorial hints, sound and the Rally integration, all through `@rally/game-kit`.                    |

## The shift

| Thing     | Rule                                                                                                        |
| --------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sawmill   | 1 plank / 1.25 s onto its tray (cap 16; stalls when full: "FULL").                                          |
| Carrying  | Pick up / drop 1 plank per 0.07 s; carry 8 (→ 16 → 28 with upgrades).                                       |
| Selling   | Needs someone at the till (you, or the cashier). Builders want 1–4 planks at $5; cart orders 8–12 at $6.    |
| Customers | Arrive every 3.0 s at the start down to 0.8 s at the end (±25 %); queue of 5; walk out after 20 s unserved. |
| Cash      | Lands on the floor pile; walk over it to collect. Pads take it while you stand on them.                     |

Unlocks, two on the ground at a time, in order: cashier $30, bigger arms $60, sharper blade $90, hauler $140, second
sawmill $320, cart orders $200, even bigger arms $260, another hauler $380. The bot earns ~$1,800 a shift, buys all
eight by about 3:40 and loses 15–25 customers in the late rush; the unit tests keep it between $1,000 and $2,500 (the
top achievement is for beating it).

## Modes and stars

Every shift is rated 0–3 stars by its earnings, a mode's stars are its best shift's, and stars added up across modes
unlock the next mode (`progress.ts`; the rules each mode changes are `MODE_RULES` in `sim.ts`). The mode picker under
the start prompt shows each mode with its stars, or how many stars it still takes; the results card shows the shift's
stars and any mode it unlocked.

| Mode      | Rules                                                                                                                    | ★ / ★★ / ★★★           | Board      | Unlocks at |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------- | ---------- | ---------- |
| Classic   | The 4-minute shift above.                                                                                                | $500 / $1,000 / $1,800 | `earnings` | –          |
| Rush Hour | 2 minutes that start 60 % of the way into the rush (a builder every 1.7 s down to 0.8 s); $50 in hand for the first pad. | $250 / $500 / $800     | `rush`     | 2 ★        |

The bot earns ~$1,800 in Classic (★★–★★★) and ~$720 in Rush Hour (★★), so three stars in Rush Hour means beating it
(that is also the `rush_hero` achievement). New modes go in `MODE_RULES` + `MODES` and a board in `game.json`.

**Outfits:** the same stars dress the player (`OUTFITS` in `progress.ts`): the Cowboy to start, the Viking at 1 ★, the
Chef at 3 ★, the Ninja at 4 ★ and the Pug suit at 6 (every star). The Outfit button at the end of the mode picker shows
what you wear and the next look still to unlock, and a tap goes round the unlocked ones (the scene rebuilds the player
rig, `setOutfit`); the results card names any outfit a shift unlocked. The Cowboy wears the player colour, the others
their own.

## Deterministic, fixed-step simulation

Everything runs in fixed 1/30 s ticks from an accumulator with a seeded RNG (mulberry32), so the same stick input per
tick gives the same camp whether frames come at 30, 60 or 120 Hz (iOS Low Power Mode halves rAF). Movers keep their
previous-tick position; the renderer interpolates with `sim.alpha`. A stall runs at most 15 ticks per frame, so game
time slows down rather than jumping. The unit test plays a bot shift at 60, 30, 120 Hz and with jittery frames and
asserts identical unlock ticks, earnings and positions. This is also the shape a Colyseus room needs for multiplayer:
the server steps the same `LumberSim` with each player's stick per tick (the rules would move to `games/lumber/server`,
as Sumo's did).

## Rally integration

Same pattern as Stack:

- **Boot:** own loading veil + `rally.ui.loading(p)` → `save.get` → first frame → `rally.ready()`; safe area as
  `--safe-*` variables.
- **Save:** slot `progress` = `{ bests: { classic, rush }, played, mode, outfit, version: 2 }` via game-kit
  `createSave` (`parseProgress` reads version 1's single `best` as the Classic one). `bests` make the stars, `mode` and
  `outfit` are the picker's choices, `played` seeds the next shift's customers and turns the first-shift tutorial hints off.
- **Shift over:** `leaderboard.submit(<mode's board>, earned)`, achievements earned this shift (`first_sale`,
  `earn_1000`, `earn_2500`, `full_camp` = all eight upgrades, `no_walkouts` = no walkouts with ≥ $500, `rush_hero` =
  $800 in Rush Hour), then that board's global top 10 for the "best · #rank" line, through game-kit `createSolo` (local fallback when there is no host).
- **Host events:** `pause` freezes `app.timeScale` + releases the stick + suspends audio + flushes the save; `resume`;
  `exitRequested` flushes then `rally.exit('host')`; `lowMemory` halves the shadow map, hides the trees and the decor
  props, and closes audio.
- **Telemetry:** `rally.telemetry.report('lumber', { fps, frameMs, p95Ms, earned, shiftTime, … })` every 10 s.

`window.__lumber` (phase, time, earned, wallet, player, unlocked, mode, stars, outfit, `autoplay(on)`, `skip(seconds)`,
`hold(on)`, `artView(view)`) exists in `vite dev` only, for the e2e and the art script.

## Models

The people and the camp props come from two **CC0** (public domain) packs by [Quaternius](https://quaternius.com): the
[Ultimate Animated Character Pack](https://quaternius.com/packs/ultimatedanimatedcharacter.html) (Cowboy_Male for the
player, Casual3_Male for the haulers, Casual2_Female for the cashier, Worker_Male and Worker_Female for the builders;
Viking_Male, Chef_Hat, Ninja_Male and Pug for the outfits)
and the [Survival Pack](https://quaternius.com/packs/survival.html) (WoodLog, Axe, Tent, Bonfire_Fire, Shovel, Backpack,
Pot). `pnpm --filter @rally/game-lumber models` rebuilds `client/src/models/` from the two zips, which it downloads from
this repo's **`art-source` release** (sha256-pinned; keep that release, or pass `MODELS_SRC=<dir with both zips>`; in
the dev container Node's fetch needs `NODE_USE_ENV_PROXY=1`), through the shared pipeline (`tooling/model-pipeline`,
runtime half in game-kit's `models.ts`):

- **Characters**: flat-colour materials become vertex colours and merge into one skinned mesh each (one draw call),
  simplified to ≈ 1,400 triangles. All nine share one skeleton (checked); `clips.glb` holds the 7 clips the game plays,
  once (Idle, Walk, Run, Walk_Carry, Run_Carry, Victory, Defeat). In the game the walk and run play at the mover's
  speed, carrying swaps in the carry walks (the plank stack sits in the arms), and the player cheers when the shift ends.
- **Recolours**: the scene recolours a copy of each mesh per look (cached): the accent material (the player's jacket,
  everyone else's shirt) and **Skin**, which the pack ships as one near-black placeholder over half the body, so each
  person gets a tone from `PALETTE.skins`.
- **Props**: one node each in `props.glb`, simplified (160–400 triangles), scaled to the camp and re-based to stand on
  y = 0; `meta.gen.ts` lists each one's size. Logs feed the mill and fill the yard; the rest dress the camp's edge and
  are hidden under `lowMemory`.

Everything generated is ≈ 440 KB gzipped (eleven `.glb.gz`, ≈ 40 KB each). The ground, mills, counter, planks, cash,
pads and trees are still primitives built in `scene.ts`.

Lumber Camp's name, camp layout, rules, text and sounds are original to Rally. The characters and props are the CC0
Quaternius models above.

## Artwork

`pnpm --filter @rally/game-lumber art` renders `art/*.webp` (listed in `game.json`) with the game itself: headless
Chromium, software WebGL, through the dev harness. The greedy bot plays a shift through the dev-only `window.__lumber`
probe (`skip` runs the seeded, fixed-step sim ahead), and `hold` stops the sim on the frame wanted while the scene keeps
animating and floating labels stay up; `artView` frames the icon and cover. `ART_ONLY=icon,cover` renders a subset.

- `icon` 1024², the player carrying a tall stack of planks from the mill to the counter (the stack is topped up for
  the picture: the bot carries small loads); `cover` 1920 × 1080, the camp mid-shift (no HUD on either).
- `shot-1` the first seconds of a shift with the tutorial hint, `shot-2` a busy camp with a sale at the till,
  `shot-3` the shift-over card (1170 × 2532, iPhone portrait @3×; the dev harness' made-up leaderboard line is left
  out).

Re-render after a visual change.
